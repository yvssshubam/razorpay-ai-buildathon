READING THE RESULT
------------------
The agent wins everywhere, but the more useful finding is the difference in
SENSITIVITY. The agent escalates 40 packets; contest-all escalates 280.

  HUMAN_REVIEW_COST  Rs 300 -> Rs 2500 (8x)
    agent        523,529 -> 503,703    (-3.8%)
    contest_all  434,315 -> -181,685   (turns negative)

  HUMAN_RESOLVE_RATE  0.2 -> 0.8 (4x)
    agent        505,799 -> 517,048    (+2.2%)
    contest_all  223,494 -> 294,315    (+31.7%)

Contest-all turns negative in three of the four sweeps: at CONTEST_COST 900,
at HUMAN_REVIEW_COST 2500, and at NET_RECOVERY_FRACTION 0.5. The agent never
does.

These four constants were chosen, not measured. The point of the sweep is that
the ordering does not depend on having chosen them correctly -- and that the
agent's rupee figure is far less exposed to them than contest-all's is. Where
the true values are unknown, that stability is itself the argument.

NET_RECOVERY_FRACTION behaves as expected: the margin compresses as recovery
improves (259,682 at 0.5 down to 207,026 at 1.0), because full recovery makes
indiscriminate contesting more attractive. The agent's advantage is largest
when recovery is poor, which is the realistic case -- industry net recovery
after second-cycle chargebacks and representment costs is reported at 12-18%
(US/global; see README "Data and its limits").
